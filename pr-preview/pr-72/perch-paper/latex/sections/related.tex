\paragraph{Capabilities and delegation.} A grant addresses a form of the
confused-deputy problem~\cite{confused-deputy}; the deputies are the account's
callees and the owner's \code{execute}, which runs with the account as invoker.
SPKI/SDSI~\cite{spki} narrows authority by intersecting tags, and
macaroons~\cite{macaroons}, Biscuit~\cite{biscuit} and UCAN~\cite{ucan} attenuate
bearer tokens offline with caveats. Perch's grants are state installed in the
account, not tokens a holder carries. A windowed cap needs past spending, which a
bearer token cannot carry without an external store; in Schneider's
terms~\cite{schneider-enforceable} a cap makes the evaluator a stateful execution
monitor, while an OZ-PB \code{read} makes a decision depend on external mutable
state, outside his history-only setting.

\paragraph{EVM account abstraction.} ERC-4337~\cite{erc4337} accounts validate
in \code{validateUserOp}; ERC-7579~\cite{erc7579} validators and hooks are the EVM
counterpart of OpenZeppelin policies; ERC-7710~\cite{erc7710} redeems
delegations, and ERC-7715~\cite{erc7715} lets an application request permissions
from a wallet. Zodiac Roles~\cite{zodiac-roles} scopes a role to targets,
functions and parameter conditions with refilling allowances, close to Perch's
targets, conditions and caps. Perch charges caps in the account's own decision;
OpenZeppelin accounts charge them in a policy contract called from
\code{\_\_check\_auth}.

\paragraph{Policy languages and synthesis.} Cedar~\cite{cedar} pairs a policy
language with formal models, and links a Lean model to its Rust implementation by
differential testing~\cite{cedar-vgd}; Perch instead proves a functional
translation of its Rust evaluator equivalent to a model, which still leaves the
compiled code trusted. All three systems deny by default: Cedar adds
\code{forbid}, Perch has no negation, and oz-policy-builder has \code{ne} and
\code{nin}, which deny when an operand does not resolve. OZ-PB's synthesis from a
recorded transaction relates to policy mining~\cite{xu-stoller-mining}.

\paragraph{Soroban verification.} Certora Sunbeam~\cite{sunbeam} checks
specifications against compiled Wasm, and Komet~\cite{komet} reasons about Wasm
with K semantics; Perch's Komet specifications check its earlier
\code{perch\_program::rpn::eval}, not \code{rule\_eval}. Both complement a proof of a
pure evaluator.
