The account document's recovery profile states the threat recovery must handle.
\emph{Loss} assumes the owner's key may be lost, not stolen: activity continues
during an attempt, and the owner, or a thief holding the key, can cancel it.
\emph{Protected} assumes theft: once the recovery condition authorizes an
attempt, the account refuses everything but its completion, and changing
recovery or upgrading needs the condition's evidence. A Protected configuration
may enroll a baseline, the hash of an approved document; a compromise recovery
restores it and revokes every credential added since. The condition is a guardian
quorum, a zero-knowledge proof, or both (modes \fact{recovery-modes};
Appendix~\ref{sec:formal-appendix}).

Anyone may open an attempt naming replacement credentials; the controller derives
the target document on chain, so nobody chooses a target hash. Each guardian
approves the SHA-256 digest of a statement binding the network, account,
controller, configuration, timing, attempt and the hashes of source, target and
replacement set. With the reference run's \fact{recovery-guardians-demo}
guardians, the second approval authorizes the attempt at ledger $A$, and
completion is allowed in \fact{recovery-window}. Completion is one transaction:
the controller's \code{rcv\_complete} confirms the approved bytes and the window,
and \code{rcv\_sync} marks the attempt completed, spends any nullifier and
advances the controller's epoch. The account applies the target, advances the
grant generation (refusing on overflow), permanently revokes the administrative
credentials the target replaced or removed, lifts any freeze and drops any pending
upgrade (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig-recovery}).

\paragraph{Ending authority.} Each earlier grant keeps its earlier generation, so
it refuses every nonempty authorization while its rules remain stored. A
credential index refuses (\code{WipedCredential}) any later document naming a
credential an earlier grant named, except the recovery target, which may keep it
until rotated. Table~\ref{tab:tab-ending} separates invalidation, a policy event,
from collection, a storage operation, and archival, which is neither.
