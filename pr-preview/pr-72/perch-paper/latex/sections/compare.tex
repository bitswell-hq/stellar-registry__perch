oz-policy-builder~\cite{oz-policy-builder} (OZ-PB) adds rules to OpenZeppelin
accounts: an off-chain synthesiser (\fact{opb-synth-package}) records a
transaction or takes a declared Intent and writes a predicate, and one shared
\code{policy-interpreter}, attached to a context rule as a policy, evaluates it in
\code{enforce} on every guarded call. Perch's deployed build
\fact{perch-deployed-source-short} used this attached-policy shape; the design
here moves the decision into the account. We compare at Perch
\fact{perch-pin-short}, OZ-PB \fact{opb-pin-short} (\fact{opb-release} and
\fact{opb-commits-after-release} later commits, none under its contracts) and
OpenZeppelin \fact{oz-v080-tag}, reading comparator design claims from source and
public documentation. Each claim states what it rests on (source, a run, tests or
a proof); these are evidence about different objects, and we do not rank them.
Appendix~\ref{sec:compare-appendix} gives twenty dimensions with sources and the
figures cited here.

\subsubsection{Per-call bounds and limits over time.}\label{sec:compare-over-time}
The interpreter decides each call alone: it does not see what a transaction
moved, keep rolling totals or count calls (OZ-PB \code{docs/stellar.md:404-407}).
The synthesiser writes a per-call bound (\code{lte}); a limit over time comes
from OpenZeppelin's optional \code{spending\_limit}, attached on \code{transfer}
only, or from a token allowance. On a local network, an OpenZeppelin account whose
rule carried only the interpreter, with a \fact{opb-per-call-bound-xlm}-XLM
per-call bound, applied \fact{opb-per-call-applied-sends} sends that moved
\fact{opb-per-call-moved-xlm}~XLM, as documented (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig-same-task}).
In Perch the window is part of the rule, charged in the decision that checks the
call, and \ref{stmt:cap-window-safety} bounds every window from empty records; a
document cannot bound a single call's amount.

\subsubsection{Authoring.}\label{sec:compare-authoring} OZ-PB starts from what an
app did: it synthesises the least predicate permitting a recorded call or an
Intent, verifies it by dry-running the deployed interpreter's own \code{check},
renders a review and returns unsigned install bytes. Perch starts from what the
owner grants: a declared document, compiled on chain and lowered against the
account's own authority (\S\ref{sec:grants}). The approaches are complementary;
a recording front end for Perch is future work (Fig.~\ref{fig:fig-two-spellings}
spells one intent both ways).

\subsubsection{Evidence.}\label{sec:compare-evidence} OZ-PB builds at
\fact{opb-pin-short}, and its \fact{opb-rust-test-runs} Rust test runs and
\fact{opb-ts-tests} TypeScript tests pass in our runs. A policy is verified by
simulating the deployed interpreter, so one evaluator verifies and enforces;
\fact{opb-tests-against-oz08} of its \fact{opb-interpreter-tests} interpreter tests
run against OpenZeppelin's \fact{oz-v080-tag} account, and it keeps a STRIDE
register. An external audit of \fact{opb-release} has been in progress since
\fact{opb-audit-start}, and its off-chain packages were hardened after an external
penetration test (pull request \fact{opb-pentest-pr}, branch
\fact{opb-pentest-branch}). Perch's evaluator is proved in Lean over its Aeneas
translation, tied to the Rust by extraction and to the account by
\fact{lean-doc-traces} traces; the theorems do not reach the Wasm, the shell is
tested, and no audit has been completed.

\subsubsection{Contexts, composition, lifecycle, binding.}\label{sec:compare-rest}
Both judge each context under its own rule (OZ-PB
\code{docs/architecture.md:307-309}; Fig.~\ref{fig:fig-contexts}); OZ-PB's
execution adapter lets one predicate relate the calls of a batch
(\code{docs/stellar.md:191-195}). An OpenZeppelin account composes a decision from
the interpreter, a threshold policy and \code{spending\_limit}; with the
interpreter alone any one authenticated signer suffices (policy-synth README,
``Threshold''). A Perch grant is one document decided by one evaluator. The
interpreter pins a policed rule to its exact signer set (error
\fact{opb-error-signers-changed} after any change), and removing a rule with only
rule~0's signatures leaves its interpreter entry until it archives
(\code{docs/stellar.md:548-562}); in Perch a completed recovery stops every earlier
grant from authorizing (\ref{stmt:recovery-invalidation}). OpenZeppelin \fact{oz-v080-tag} binds the
account and rule IDs for both signer kinds; Perch's delegated signers do not bind
rule IDs (Table~\ref{tab:tab-binding}).

\subsubsection{The walkthrough run.}\label{sec:compare-run} In one soroban-sim
harness both designs gave the same verdict on each of seven requests
(Table~\ref{tab:tab-walkthrough}). The OpenZeppelin account called
\code{spending\_limit} and the interpreter, and its delegated signer approved in a
second authorization entry; Perch called its verifier. The signer kinds differ,
because OZ-PB takes only delegated signers. Over the cap, \code{spending\_limit},
first by address here, refused before the interpreter ran; Perch refused before
any verifier call. This is one task, not a cost comparison; W1--W5 are proposed,
not run (Appendix~\ref{sec:compare-appendix}), and the \fact{oz-v072-tag}-line
account build OZ-PB signs for has no recorded source.
