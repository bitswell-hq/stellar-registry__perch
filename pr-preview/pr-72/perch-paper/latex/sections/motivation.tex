A player keeps a Perch account in \fact{wallet-name} and wants Guess the Number,
a Soroban game that takes \fact{game-wrong-guess-xlm}~XLM per wrong guess and
pays out its balance on a right one, to play without a passkey prompt per turn.
The owner can install a \emph{grant} naming the contract and functions an app may
call, the signer that may act, how much it may spend over time and when it ends;
it must not let the app reach the account's own entry points or its recovery
controller. In the reference run (\S\ref{sec:evaluation}), grant~1 lets an app
transfer at most \fact{grant1-cap-xlm}~XLM in any
\fact{grant1-period-ledgers}-ledger window (\fact{grant1-period-minutes} at the
nominal \fact{nominal-ledger-seconds}-second ledger), and grant~3 lets the app key
call the game's \code{guess} and lets the game take at most
\fact{grant3-cap-xlm}~XLM per \fact{grant3-period-ledgers} ledgers from the
account, so two lost guesses fit and a third is refused.

\paragraph{Problem and approach.} A grant needs one meaning across the nested
calls of one authorization, which Soroban asks the account to decide one at a
time; under rule selection, which whoever builds the transaction performs; and
across recovery, which replaces the credentials a grant was issued under. A cap
also needs state. Perch makes the decision a pure state transition, linked into
the account and run before any signer is authenticated, and separates what is
proved about it from the obligations of the account around it
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fig-parts}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
\item A Soroban smart-account design whose authorization is a pure state
  transition over the selected rules, account-held spending records and a grant
  generation. Grant lowering excludes the account, its adopted recovery
  controller, and the zero-knowledge adapter and pool of its recovery
  configuration (\S\S\ref{sec:grants}--\ref{sec:authorization}).
\item A grant generation that a completed recovery advances: afterwards no earlier
  grant authorizes, nor, in the account model, any grant derived from one, and a
  credential index bars their keys from later documents other than a completion's
  target (\S\ref{sec:recovery}).
\item Lean theorems over the Aeneas translation of the Rust evaluator, with an
  explicit trust base and \fact{lean-doc-traces} Lean-generated traces that
  connect them to the account (\S\ref{sec:proofs}).
\item A source-cited comparison with oz-policy-builder, with one task run in both
  designs (\S\ref{sec:compare}), and an evaluation on a local Soroban host and on
  testnet (\S\ref{sec:evaluation}). Comparative cost measurement is proposed.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Research questions.} \textbf{RQ1}: does the built account enforce
grants, caps and recovery invalidation as designed? \textbf{RQ2}: which parts of
a decision are proved, tested or trusted? \textbf{RQ3}: what does a decision cost
on chain, for Perch alone? \textbf{RQ4}: how does a linked evaluator differ from
an attached shared interpreter? \S\ref{sec:evaluation} answers RQ1 and RQ3,
\S\ref{sec:proofs} RQ2 and \S\ref{sec:compare} RQ4. The design is an open
pull-request stack, previewed on Stellar testnet (\S\ref{sec:status}).
