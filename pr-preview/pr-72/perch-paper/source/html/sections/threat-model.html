We consider two attackers. The first is the app itself, or someone who stole its key. Under the shop grant, that key can send at most {{fact:shop-cap-xlm}} XLM per {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers to the shop and nothing else: no other token, recipient or function, and no call to the account. The second submits or relays transactions. It chooses when to submit and can resubmit a refused request until it expires, but it cannot change what an external signer's signature covers.

The account trusts the Soroban host, the verifier contracts a grant names, the document compiler, its adopted recovery controller and the wallet's display of what is signed. It assumes the signature schemes cannot be forged and SHA-256 is collision resistant.

What a grant does not cover

  • A key named by several rules holds all of them, and each cap applies to one rule, not to the key or the grant.
  • Replacing a capped rule starts an empty window, so lowering a cap right after the app spent up to it allows the new limit again in the same window.
  • A standing token allowance and the owner's execute, which calls any contract as the account, move value outside any grant, and a rule on a token contract with no function list also allows approve.
  • A delegated signer's approval does not fix the rule, and a rule edited between signing and inclusion decides under its new contents.
  • A spending record holds at most {{fact:spends-per-window}} spends, and a grant document at most {{fact:doc-max-rules}} rules and {{fact:doc-max-signers}} signers.
