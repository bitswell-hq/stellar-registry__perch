A grant has to hold against the app that holds it, against whoever submits the transaction, and against a thief of the owner's key.

{{ref:tab:tab-adversaries}} lists five adversaries, what each can do, what Perch claims against each with its evidence, and what remains. Proved means a Lean theorem checked by Lean's kernel ({{ref:sec:proofs}}); tested means tests of the built contracts. {{ref:sec:limits}} discusses each residual.

{{table:tab-adversaries}}

Goals

  • G1 Containment. No grant rule allows a call to the account, its adopted recovery controller, or the zero-knowledge adapter and pool of its recovery configuration. Proved for lowering under Doc.Valid (T8, T9); the runtime recheck is tested.
  • G2 Permission soundness. An accepted authorization is allowed by the rules it cites. Proved for the evaluator (T2); signer authentication, rule selection and context conversion are tested.
  • G3 Window safety. A capped rule's charges in any window stay within its limit. Proved for the evaluator from empty records (T6); rule selection and record persistence are tested; on chain a window holds at most {{fact:spends-per-window}} spends.
  • G4 Recovery invalidation. After a completed recovery, no earlier grant authorizes through the account; allowances it created stay usable ({{ref:sec:limits}}). Shown in the account model (T10), by shell tests, by the executed reference run and in the testnet record.
  • G5 Signature binding. An external signer's signature binds the account, the entry and the selected rule IDs. Tested, with delegated signers excluded; the academic edition gives a paper proof.
  • G6 Fail-closed refusal. A refusal writes nothing. Tested; in the model a refusal yields no next state.

In Saltzer and Schroeder's terms {{cite:saltzer-schroeder}}, __check_auth mediates every require_auth on the account that the host satisfies from an authorization entry; one satisfied by invoker authority, as in the owner's execute, never reaches it ({{ref:sec:limits}}). Not goals: budgets shared across grants; effects outside authorization, such as token allowances, the owner's execute and funds held by other contracts; confidentiality; the correctness of verifiers; and what a wallet displays.
