A Perch grant is a set of rules installed in the account and decided by a pure evaluator linked into it, before any signer is authenticated, with spending records kept in the account's own storage. The evaluator's core properties are proved in Lean over its translation, and the account shell around it is tested. That a completed recovery stops every earlier grant from authorizing is proved in the account model, tested, executed in this page and recorded on testnet. Against oz-policy-builder, the designs differ in where a limit over time lives (a window inside Perch's decision; a per-call bound beside OpenZeppelin's optional spending_limit), in how a rule is written (a declared grant; a predicate synthesised from a recording or an Intent), and in the evidence behind each (kernel-checked proofs about the evaluator's translation; tests, partly on the shipped Wasm, and an audit in progress). What remains is merging the open pull requests, an external audit, and measuring Perch and oz-policy-builder with exact metering on more workloads than the one task run here.
