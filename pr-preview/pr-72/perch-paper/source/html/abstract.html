ABSTRACT

Perch is a smart account for Stellar's Soroban platform. Its owner can give an app a grant: a permission that says which contracts and functions the app may call, which arguments are allowed, how much it may spend in a sliding window of ledgers, and when the permission ends. The account checks each call against the grant before it checks any signature, and keeps the spending records in its own storage. If the owner recovers the account after losing a key, one counter moves and every earlier grant stops authorizing in that same transaction. A toolchain translates the Rust rule check into the Lean proof assistant, where machine-checked proofs show that it matches a readable model, accepts only calls the selected rules allow, and, from empty spending records, keeps each cap within its limit in every window; the rest of the account is tested. We compare Perch with oz-policy-builder, which adds rules to OpenZeppelin smart accounts through a shared on-chain interpreter. A preview of the Nido wallet ran these contracts on Stellar testnet, and this page runs them in your browser.

At a glance

  1. A grant is a bounded permission the owner installs in the account. Its rules name a contract, the functions and arguments allowed, who signs, when it ends and how much it may spend ({{ref:fig:fig-contexts}}).
  2. The account checks the grant before any signature, and keeps its own spending records. No separate policy contract takes part in the decision ({{ref:fig:fig-two-paths}}).
  3. Caps slide. A cap of L per P ledgers lets one rule charge at most L in any P consecutive ledgers; replacing the rule starts a new window ({{ref:fig:fig-cap-window}}, {{ref:sec:limits}}).
  4. A completed recovery stops every earlier grant from authorizing, in one transaction. One counter moves, and grants stamped with the old value refuse ({{ref:fig:fig-recovery}}).
  5. The rule check is proved in Lean; the rest is tested. The proofs are about a translation of the Rust, not the compiled contract ({{ref:fig:fig-proof-paths}}).
