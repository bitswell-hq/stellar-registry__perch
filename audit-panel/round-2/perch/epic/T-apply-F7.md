## T-apply-F7 — The delta oracle shares compile/desired() with the code under test, and storage_state hides ordering, so the 'authorization equals a full replace' evidence cannot catch defects there

- Source: T-apply (finding)
- Jev improves: 0.94
- Jev defect / severity: requirement_or_code / medium
- Locations: nido/vendor/perch/crates/perch-testkit/src/delta.rs:1-20, nido/vendor/perch/crates/perch-testkit/src/delta.rs:190-390, nido/vendor/perch/crates/perch-smart-account/src/lib.rs:932-951, nido/vendor/perch/docs/audit-scope.md:171-176

OracleAccount runs the same apply() with Mode::FullReplace, so the expiry drop in rules::desired, the recovery-rule synthesis and compile_doc are on both sides of every comparison. The generator (DocModel::generate) never advances the ledger (NOT_AFTER is 5-7 million with the ledger at 1000), never permutes signer lists (subset() is sorted), stays below the caps (<=6 signers, <=7 rules) and never uses uppercase hex or non-canonical numbers. The audit scope cites this fuzz target and apply_delta.rs as the evidence that a delta apply grants exactly what a full replace grants.

**Recommendation:** Extend DocModel with ledger advances across near-term not-after bounds, signer permutations and cap-sized documents (audit_delta_edges.rs::delta_matches_full_replace_under_permutation_and_time does this and passes), and compare records against get_context_rule. State in audit-scope.md that the oracle shares desired()/compile.
