## R-docs-F5 — Ordinary-signature rule binding is overstated for delegated signers

- Source: R-docs (finding)
- Jev improves: 1.00
- Jev defect / severity: description_only / medium
- Locations: nido/vendor/perch/docs/recovery/spec.md:1572-1586, nido/vendor/perch/docs/DEFERRED.md:123-143, nido/ARCHITECTURE.md:220-225, nido/docs/THREAT_MODEL.md:185-196, nido/vendor/perch/docs/recovery/cap-0071.md:28, nido/vendor/perch/crates/integration-tests/tests/cap71_semantics.rs:449-482

The normative specification and deferral register say an ordinary signature binds the invocation and selected rule IDs, and rely on removing a selected rule to make an outstanding signature fail closed. Nido repeats this claim in its architecture and threat model. This is true for External signers, but not for Signer::Delegated. CAP-0071 C4 states the opposite, and its enforcing test selects either of two rules while demonstrating that the delegate receives the identical payload. The rule-ID selection can be changed without changing the delegate's approval.

**Recommendation:** Qualify the rule-ID-binding and fail-closed statements to External signatures. Include the C4 exception in the normative revision-binding section and deferral rationale, describing re-selection among surviving rules containing the same delegate.
