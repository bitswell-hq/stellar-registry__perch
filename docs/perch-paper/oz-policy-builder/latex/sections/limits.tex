\paragraph{What was run.} Every comparator claim is read from source and public
documentation at the pins, except the results of our runs (\S\ref{sec:overview}).
Both runs of the task are one task, on soroban-sim's recording-mode host build
without exact metering, with different signer kinds on the two sides; they show
verdicts and call structure, not cost.

\paragraph{Proposed measurement.} We propose, and have not run, five workloads
under one harness with exact metering, one host version and Ed25519 signers on
both sides, reporting CPU instructions, memory, ledger I/O, fees and
cross-contract calls: W1, one capped transfer; W2, a losing guess and the
transfer it makes; W3, one grant install; W4, ending authority over $k$ grants
(one recovery against $k$ calls to \code{remove\_context\_rule}); W5, storage
after $n$ transfers.

\paragraph{What we do not claim.} Until W1--W5 run, we claim no difference in
calls, cost, speed or size, and compare no code sizes. We do not claim that Perch
binds more in general, or that oz-policy-builder is unproven (we found no formal
model in its tree) or runs on mainnet (OpenZeppelin's \fact{oz-v072-tag} policies
do). We do not claim that \code{spending\_limit} and Perch's cap behave alike
beyond their shared eviction boundary. Items oz-policy-builder discloses about
features Perch lacks (Table~\ref{tab:tab-disclosed}) are not security properties
of Perch.
