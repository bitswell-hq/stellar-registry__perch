The decision function is about {{fact:rule-eval-lines}} lines of Rust. The Charon {{cite:charon}} and Aeneas {{cite:aeneas}} tools translate it into Lean {{cite:lean4}}, a proof assistant whose kernel checks every proof, and theorems relate the translation to a readable model written by hand. rule-core's CI regenerates the translation and fails on any difference.

  • The translation behaves as the model does. On every input, the Lean translation of the Rust returns exactly what the model returns, without panicking or looping. If the model refuses the shop grant's second payment of {{fact:sim-second-pay-xlm}} XLM, so does the translated code.
  • An accepted call is always allowed by a rule. If the decision function accepts a request, every call in it is allowed by a rule it was given, under the signers it was given. Under the shop grant, every accepted call is a transfer from the account to the shop that the app's key signed.
  • No window holds more than the cap. From an empty record, however the app times its payments, no window of {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers holds more than {{fact:shop-cap-xlm}} XLM of accepted charges. {{ref:fig:fig-cap-window}} is one instance: 6 and 4 XLM fill the window, 1 XLM at ledger t + 99 is refused, and 5 XLM at t + 100 is accepted because the 6 XLM charged at t has left the window.
  • No grant reaches the account itself. No installed grant, and no grant derived from one, allows a call to the account or to the recovery contracts it has adopted. The account refuses at install any rule that targets it, so an app cannot use its grant to install a wider one.

{{ref:fig:fig-proof-scope}} draws the boundary. The theorems are about the Lean translation, not the compiled WebAssembly the account runs, and nothing checks one against the other. The account code around the decision function is tested: Perch's tests run {{fact:lean-doc-traces}} traces generated from the Lean model through the natively compiled account and compare every verdict and spending record. The proofs also trust Lean's kernel, the two translators, rustc's intermediate representation and the hand-written specification, and nothing is audited.

The formal version. What Perch proves states all {{fact:statements-count}} theorems with their definitions, hypotheses and Lean names; its Theorems 1, 2, 6 and 8 are the four results above. The PDF edition gives them in mathematical form.
