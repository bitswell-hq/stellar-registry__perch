A grant is a set of rules. Each names one contract, the functions and arguments it allows and who must sign, and may add an expiry and a cap on spending in a sliding window.

The owner writes a grant as a JSON grant document and installs it with one call to the account, apply_scope (the code calls a grant a scope), after a document compiler contract validates it. A rule allows a call when the called contract is its target, the function is on its list, every argument condition holds, such as "argument 1 is the shop", and enough of its listed signers signed. A rule may also stop at an expiry ledger and, when its only function is transfer, carry a cap.

The cap counts a sliding window

A cap is a limit and a period, such as {{fact:shop-cap-xlm}} XLM per {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers. At any ledger the window is the last {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers, and a transfer is accepted only if the window's total plus the new amount stays within the limit. The account keeps the charges in a spending record in its own storage and charges a transfer in the same decision that checks it, so there is no separate step to skip.

For the same cap and period, a sliding window is a tighter limit than one that resets on a schedule. MetaMask's delegation framework {{cite:metamask-delegation}} caps token transfers per fixed period that resets at each period's start (ERC20PeriodTransferEnforcer.sol:11-16), so an app can spend the full cap just before a reset and again just after it. A Perch window starting from an empty record stays within its limit wherever it is placed.

A grant ends by expiry, removal or recovery

A grant's rules stop at their expiry, and the owner can remove a grant with one call, remove_scope, which deletes its rules and spending records. If the owner recovers the account after losing a key, one counter on the account advances and every earlier grant stops authorizing in that transaction. Token allowances a grant created stay usable after it ends.
