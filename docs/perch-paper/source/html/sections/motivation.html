A grant lets an app or agent act for an account within limits the account itself enforces.

On Stellar, a smart account is a contract that holds a user's funds and decides which calls may act in its name; Soroban is the platform that runs such contracts. An app that spends for a user today has two choices: ask the user to approve every call, or hold a key that can do anything the account can. Neither suits an agent that pays a service many times an hour. Perch adds a third. The owner installs a grant, a written and bounded permission, and the account checks against it every call the app makes in the account's name. {{ref:fig:fig-overview}} shows who does what.

The shop grant

The running example lets an app send XLM, Stellar's native currency, from the account to one shop, at most {{fact:shop-cap-xlm}} XLM in any {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers ({{ref:fig:fig-shop-grant}}). A ledger is one closed block of the network, nominally {{fact:nominal-ledger-seconds}} seconds apart, so {{fact:shop-period-ledgers}} ledgers is {{fact:shop-period-minutes}}. The grant has one rule. It names the contract the app may call (the XLM contract), the function (transfer), a condition on the recipient, the key that must sign (the app's), and a cap: a limit on the total sent in a sliding window of ledgers. The simulation in {{ref:sec:simulation}} installs this grant.

{{fig:fig-shop-grant}}

What a grant cannot say

  • A bound on one call's amount. The cap bounds the total in the window, so one call may spend the whole limit.
  • Or and not. Every condition in a rule must hold.
  • Reads of other contracts, such as a price oracle.
  • How calls relate. The account checks each call alone, so a rule meant for a payment a game makes on the user's behalf also allows the same payment made directly ({{ref:sec:background}}, {{ref:fig:fig-contexts}}).

The claim, word by word

This paper argues one claim: safe, bounded delegation for apps and agents on Stellar, simple, proved, and testable end to end in soroban-sim. Each word has a narrow meaning here.

  • Safe and bounded mean three properties, proved for the decision function and for lowering, with the account code around them tested; they do not mean audited or ready for real funds. Each call of an accepted request is allowed by some installed rule ({{ref:stmt:acceptance-implies-permission}}); no window of a capped rule holds more than its limit ({{ref:stmt:cap-window-safety}}); and no grant allows a call to the account itself or its adopted recovery contracts ({{ref:stmt:grant-target-containment}}).
  • Simple means one document, one install call (apply_scope) and one decision point. The cost is fewer conditions than oz-policy-builder, a rule builder for OpenZeppelin's Soroban accounts, offers ({{ref:sec:compare}}).
  • Proved applies to the translation of the decision function into Lean, a proof assistant whose kernel checks every proof. It does not apply to the compiled contract; the code around the function is tested ({{ref:sec:proofs}}).
  • End to end covers the contract code of both designs, run together in soroban-sim, a portable Soroban host: Perch's account, verifier and document compiler; OpenZeppelin's account and spending_limit; oz-policy-builder's interpreter; and the XLM contract. Wallets, RPC servers, passkeys and fees are outside it.

Grants also end with the account's recovery: if the owner recovers the account after losing a key, one counter moves and every earlier grant stops authorizing through the account in that transaction (the vision paper).
