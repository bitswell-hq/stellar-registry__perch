A grant lets an app act for an account without the owner approving each call; this paper is about what a grant means and how the account enforces it.

Suppose a player keeps XLM in a Perch account: a Soroban contract that holds the player's funds and decides which calls it authorizes in the player's name. The player opens Guess the Number {{cite:guess-the-number}}, a Soroban game that takes {{fact:game-wrong-guess-xlm}} XLM for each wrong guess and pays out its balance on a right one, and wants the game to play without a passkey prompt on every turn. The owner can install a grant: a permission, stored in the account, that says which contract may be called, which function may run, which arguments are acceptable, which key must sign, how much may be spent over time, and when the permission ends. It should not let the game call the account's own entry points or its recovery controller, the separate contract that runs the account's recovery.

The paper follows three grants that the live experiment installs:

  • Grant 1, the demo's “original app”, may transfer at most {{fact:grant1-cap-xlm}} XLM in any {{fact:grant1-period-ledgers}}-ledger window, {{fact:grant1-period-minutes}} at the nominal {{fact:nominal-ledger-seconds}}-second ledger. A {{fact:grant1-spend-applied-xlm}}-XLM transfer is applied; a further {{fact:grant1-spend-refused-xlm}} XLM in the same window is refused.
  • Grant 2 gives that app a fresh key after the owner recovers the account.
  • Grant 3 lets the game's app key call guess, and lets the game take at most {{fact:grant3-cap-xlm}} XLM per {{fact:grant3-period-ledgers}} ledgers from the account, so two lost guesses fit and a third is refused.

Two further requirements shape the design. A grant derived from another, for example one an agent hands to a sub-agent ({{ref:sec:agents}}), must allow nothing its parent does not. And if the owner loses a key and recovers the account, the completed recovery must end the earlier grants' authority at once, before anyone collects their storage. (Once a grant has stopped authorizing, anyone may delete its stored records; the paper calls this collection, {{ref:sec:recovery}}.)

Perch makes the decision a pure function. Given the selected rules, their spending records, the calls, the claimed signers, the ledger number and a counter called the grant generation, it either refuses or returns the next spending records, and it touches nothing else. That function is small enough to translate into the Lean proof assistant and prove properties of ({{ref:sec:proofs}}). The account shell around it, the contract code that stores state and checks signatures, is tested rather than proved, and the paper keeps the two apart.

The parts of a Perch account

{{ref:fig:fig-parts}} shows the parts at the pinned build. The owner signs with a passkey through a wallet; an app signs with its own grant key. For each authorization the Soroban host calls the account's __check_auth. Inside the account, the linked library rule_eval decides authorizations (authorize) and turns documents into stored rules (lower, lower_scoped). Every rule, grant and spending record lives in the account's own storage. Calls leave the account to a verifier contract on every authorization, to the document compiler on every install, to the recovery controller on account-document writes, upgrades, cancellations and completions, to the zero-knowledge pool at enrollment, and to any contract through an owner-authorized execute.
