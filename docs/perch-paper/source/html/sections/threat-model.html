A grant has to hold against the app that holds it and against whoever submits its transactions.

We consider two adversaries ({{ref:tab:tab-grantee}}). A1 is the grantee: the app or agent key, or whoever steals it. It can sign anything within the rules that name its key, build any call tree, and choose among those rules. A2 submits or relays transactions: it wraps signed entries, chooses timing and fee, and can resubmit a refused entry until it expires. Below, proved means a Lean theorem checked by Lean's kernel, and tested means tests of the built contracts.

{{table:tab-grantee}}

Goals

  • G1 Containment. No grant rule allows a call to the account, its adopted recovery controller, or the zero-knowledge adapter and pool of its recovery configuration. Proved for lowering ({{ref:stmt:grant-target-containment}}); the account's recheck at authorization time is tested.
  • G2 Permission soundness. An accepted request is allowed by the rules it names. Proved for the decision function ({{ref:stmt:acceptance-implies-permission}}); signer authentication is tested.
  • G3 Window safety. A capped rule's charges in any window stay within its limit. Proved from empty records ({{ref:stmt:cap-window-safety}}); rule selection and record persistence are tested.
  • G6 Fail-closed refusal. A refusal writes nothing. Tested; in the model a refusal yields no next state.

G4, recovery invalidation, is proved in an account model ({{ref:stmt:recovery-invalidation}}), tested and run on testnet; it and G5, signature binding, are set out in the vision paper.

Trusted: the Soroban host and validators; the verifier contracts that documents name; the adopted recovery controller; the document compiler; rustc; the wallet's display of what is signed; and, for the proofs, Lean's kernel, the Charon and Aeneas translators, and the specification.

Not goals: budgets shared across grants; effects outside authorization, such as token allowances, the owner's execute and funds held by other contracts; confidentiality; the correctness of verifiers; and what a wallet displays. Theft of the owner's key and the components the account trusts (A3–A5) are covered in the vision paper.
