oz-policy-builder adds rules to OpenZeppelin accounts through a shared on-chain interpreter; Perch decides inside the account. Each can express things the other cannot.

This section compares Perch with oz-policy-builder {{cite:oz-policy-builder}}, release line {{fact:opb-release}}, at {{fact:opb-pin-short}}. Claims about its design are read from its source at that pin. Where we ran it, the text says so: we built it, ran its test suites and its install flow, and ran one task through both designs. Several words mean different things in the two systems (grant, scope, policy, interpreter, execute, recovery, cap, expiry); the glossary gives both meanings.

Two places to decide

oz-policy-builder has two halves. Off chain, a synthesiser ({{fact:opb-synth-package}}, with a command-line tool and an MCP server that expose {{fact:opb-tools-count}} tools) records a transaction or takes a declared Intent, writes a predicate in the interpreter's wire form, dry-runs it with check, renders a review, and returns an unsigned install transaction. On chain, one shared policy-interpreter contract, attached to an OpenZeppelin context rule as a policy, evaluates the stored predicate in enforce on every guarded call. Much of its docs/stellar.md describes Prime, a custody product built on these parts; the interpreter and the synthesiser are the parts that correspond to Perch's engine.

Perch's deployed build {{fact:perch-deployed-source-short}} used this attached-policy shape on a fork of OpenZeppelin's account library; the design in this paper moves the decision into the account.

Three more ideas, from oz-policy-builder

  • Predicate. The interpreter's stored expression for one (account, rule) pair. It denies by default and has no not; its ne and nin deny when an operand does not resolve.
  • Execution adapter. A contract that runs a batch of calls atomically, because Soroban allows one host-function invocation per transaction. A predicate on its execute can relate the calls in that batch.
  • Keyless rule. A rule with no signer. The interpreter admits one when it is bound to an execution adapter; anyone may then run what the predicate permits. Perch refuses a rule with no signer (UnguardedRule).

{{ref:fig:fig-two-paths}} runs one guarded call through both accounts, phase by phase, for three scenarios: a lost guess, a guess over the cap, and a direct transfer. Perch makes its whole decision, caps included, before any signer is authenticated, and writes the spending record in the account. The OpenZeppelin account selects one rule per context, refuses a signer that is on no selected rule, and authenticates the signers; a delegated signer approves through its own, second authorization entry. It then runs each attached policy's enforce in contract-address order (OpenZeppelin storage.rs:830), so whether the interpreter or spending_limit runs first depends on their addresses. Over the cap, Perch refuses in its decision with auth error {{fact:auth-error-rule-eval-refused}}, and the OpenZeppelin account refuses in spending_limit with SpendingLimitExceeded ({{fact:oz-spending-limit-exceeded}}). Two ways to say the same thing

{{ref:fig:fig-two-spellings}} writes one intent in both systems. The intent is the game grant that Nido's testnet preview installed, not the demo's grant 3: it pins the recipient, and it has a {{fact:preview-game-period-ledgers}}-ledger window and an expiry. The numbered list matches the marks in the figure.

{{fig:fig-two-spellings}}
  1. Both systems select one rule per context, and each rule sees its own call alone (oz-policy-builder's docs/architecture.md:307-309; {{ref:fig:fig-contexts}}, case c). So in both, a transfer to the game is allowed without a guess.
  2. In Perch the window cap is part of the rule. The interpreter keeps no rolling totals; OpenZeppelin's spending_limit, attached beside it, is the opt-in for a window (docs/stellar.md:404-407). oz-policy-builder's builder also requires a per-call bound (lte; it refuses an uncapped amount unless allowUnboundedAmount is set). That bound has no lower bound; oz-policy-builder's notes for rule writers advise adding one, because Stellar amounts are signed (docs/architecture.md:305-306). A Perch document cannot write a per-call bound below the window limit ({{ref:sec:grants}}).
  3. Perch installs both rules in one transaction. oz-policy-builder builds one add_context_rule per install_policy call (read from source; this example's installs were not run).
  4. Perch's grant rule holds a passkey as an external signer. oz-policy-builder writes every rule signer as Delegated, and the interpreter refuses External signers (error {{fact:opb-error-external-signer}}). An oz-policy-builder app therefore signs as a Stellar or contract address, in its own authorization entry, and sourceAccount must be a signer of rule 0.
  5. Expiry: {{fact:perch-expiry-semantics}}. OpenZeppelin's valid_until authorizes through itself, so validUntilLedger: N − 1 names the same last ledger.
  6. oz-policy-builder can consult live state, for example a read of a registry before permitting a recipient; a Perch rule reads nothing outside the account. A Perch grant ends when the account recovers; oz-policy-builder has no account-key recovery, and the “recovery” of its Prime product moves custody funds to a fixed address.

What does “minimal” mean when synthesis starts from a recording rather than an Intent? A recorded lost guess would pin guess's arguments to the recorded values, because guess is not in the synthesiser's registry (derived from source, not run). And a synthesiser that reads one transaction cannot see totals, expiry, relations among calls, effects outside the authorization tree, or other rules.

One task, run in both designs

We ran one task through both designs in soroban-sim: {{fact:wt-task}}. The OpenZeppelin account carries the rule oz-policy-builder's synthesiser writes for that task, with OpenZeppelin's spending_limit beside the interpreter; the Perch account carries one grant. {{ref:tab:tab-walkthrough}} lists the requests. The two designs gave the same verdict on every request and differ in where they refuse. A key that signs in the app's name without being the app's key fails in the host's signature check in both: on the OpenZeppelin side before any policy runs, on the Perch side after the evaluator accepts, when the verifier traps. In this deployment spending_limit ran before the interpreter, so over the cap the interpreter was not called.

What each does that the other does not

oz-policy-builder and the OpenZeppelin account, not Perch

  • disjunction, ordering, quantifiers and arithmetic in a predicate, and ledger-time conditions;
  • cross-contract reads, such as oracles and registries;
  • predicates over a whole execution-adapter batch;
  • per-call amount bounds;
  • keyless rules bound to an executor;
  • rules that follow another account's rule;
  • a separate policy-admin role;
  • an install nonce;
  • a specific deny code for each reason;
  • binding of rule IDs for delegated signers;
  • synthesis from a recorded transaction, with minimality checks, an overlap scan and an owner-side review of unsigned bytes;
  • an external audit in progress.

Perch, not oz-policy-builder

  • a windowed cap inside the decision;
  • the decision before authentication;
  • all decision state in one contract;
  • invalidation of every grant by one counter at recovery;
  • account-key recovery;
  • passkey (External) signers on grant rules;
  • one install for a grant that spans contracts;
  • an attenuation model;
  • kernel-checked proofs of the evaluator.

Assurance and status

  • Neither external audit is complete. oz-policy-builder's audit of {{fact:opb-release}} has been {{fact:opb-audit-status}} since {{fact:opb-audit-start}}, and its scope excludes the OpenZeppelin account and policies. Perch has had none.
  • oz-policy-builder tests the shipped Wasm: {{fact:opb-interpreter-tests}} interpreter tests, {{fact:opb-tests-against-oz08}} of them against OpenZeppelin's {{fact:oz-v080-tag}} account, and a worst-case suite measured on Wasm. Perch's theorems do not reach the Wasm, and its {{fact:lean-doc-traces}} Lean-generated traces run against natively registered contracts.
  • We built oz-policy-builder at {{fact:opb-pin-short}}; its {{fact:opb-rust-test-runs}} Rust test runs and {{fact:opb-ts-tests}} TypeScript tests pass. We ran its record, synthesise, verify and install flow against an OpenZeppelin {{fact:oz-v080-tag}} account on {{fact:opb-local-network}}, and every call outside the rule was refused with the code its documentation gives.
  • oz-policy-builder keeps a STRIDE threat register with a control and a status for each threat. We found no formal model in its tree, which is the result of a search, not a proof of absence. Perch's evaluator has kernel-checked proofs over its translation ({{ref:sec:proofs}}).
  • oz-policy-builder pins an interpreter on testnet and none on mainnet; the OpenZeppelin {{fact:oz-v072-tag}} policies are on mainnet. This paper's design runs on testnet as a preview, and Perch's earlier design is on mainnet ({{ref:sec:status}}).

What this comparison does not show

  • Design claims about oz-policy-builder are read from source at the pins; the runs above cover its test suites, its install flow and the one task in {{ref:tab:tab-walkthrough}}.
  • The walkthrough's counts come from a recording-mode host in one harness, for one task, on two different contract stacks. Fees, memory, storage and exact-metered costs on both designs, over more workloads, are proposed ({{ref:sec:agents}}).
  • The OpenZeppelin 0.7.2-line account build that oz-policy-builder signs for ({{fact:oz-v072-line-account-build}}) has no recorded source.
