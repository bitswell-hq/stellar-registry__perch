An OpenZeppelin account with oz-policy-builder composes its decision from separate contracts; Perch makes it at one point. oz-policy-builder expresses more conditions and is under external audit; Perch keeps the limit over time inside the rule.

In OpenZeppelin's Soroban account library {{cite:oz-stellar-accounts}}, the unit of permission is a context rule: a target such as one contract, an optional expiry, signers, and attached contracts, each a policy the account calls on every call the rule approves. oz-policy-builder {{cite:oz-policy-builder}} writes rules for these accounts. It synthesises a predicate, a condition on one call, from a recorded transaction or a declared intent. A shared policy contract, the interpreter, evaluates the predicate on every call the rule guards. Its README calls the interpreter "one audit-once engine", because a new combination of constraints is data, not a new contract (README.md:13-24).

A limit over time is part of a Perch rule and an add-on in OpenZeppelin

The interpreter judges one call at a time and keeps no totals. oz-policy-builder's documentation says limits over time come from a token allowance with an expiry, or from OpenZeppelin's spending_limit (docs/stellar.md:404-407), an opt-in policy beside the interpreter that accepts only transfer. In Perch the window is part of the rule and is charged in the same decision that checks the call. {{ref:fig:fig-compose}} models both arrangements; detach the window cap in (b) and payments that (a) refuses go through.

{{fig:fig-compose}}

So a Perch cap is part of its rule. It cannot be detached on its own; removing it means replacing the rule. A reviewer reads one document and one decision function, rather than the account's checks plus every policy attached to each rule. One owner call removes a grant across every contract it covers, where an OpenZeppelin account's rules are removed one at a time.

Each design is stronger somewhere

oz-policy-builder

  • Maturity. Its interpreter is pinned on testnet, the project is under external audit, its off-chain packages were hardened after a penetration test, and it is exercised on recorded mainnet transactions.
  • Authoring. It tests that each condition of a synthesised predicate is needed and verifies the predicate against the deployed interpreter; a CLI and an MCP server expose its {{fact:opb-tools-count}} tools to shells and agents.
  • Expressiveness and reach. Or, comparisons, conditions over lists, clock windows, slippage floors, reads of other contracts, threshold policies and a deny code for each reason. Its installer supports existing OpenZeppelin account builds, including the 0.7.2 line in use on mainnet (policy-synth/README.md:143).

Perch

  • Limits over time. Every capped rule's decision includes its window.
  • One decision point. One contract holds the rules and their state, installs one document that covers several contracts, and accepts passkey signers on grant rules.
  • Proved decision logic. The decision function is proved in Lean to accept only what the rules allow and to keep each window within its cap.

A Perch grant needs a Perch account.

Future work borrows four ideas from oz-policy-builder

As future work, we plan recording-based authoring, a front end that turns a recorded transaction into a grant document for review; simulating a grant against the account's own decision function before install; refusing a capped rule that the same key could bypass through another rule, as oz-policy-builder's installer does (policy-synth/README.md:171); and testing against a corpus of real mainnet transactions.

The companion paper Perch and oz-policy-builder sets out every dimension with a source for each cell, and its walkthrough replays one task in both designs step by step.
