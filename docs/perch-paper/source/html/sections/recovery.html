A completed recovery moves one counter, and every grant installed before it stops authorizing in the same transaction.

The account document chooses a recovery profile, which fixes the threat recovery addresses. Loss assumes the owner's key may be lost but not stolen: ordinary activity, grants included, continues during an attempt, and the owner can cancel any attempt at any time, so a thief holding the owner key can cancel every recovery. Protected assumes the key may be stolen: once the recovery condition authorizes an attempt, the account refuses everything except its completion, and changing the recovery configuration or upgrading the account needs the condition's evidence. The condition is a guardian quorum, a zero-knowledge proof, or both, in the modes {{fact:recovery-modes}}.

The live experiment uses {{fact:recovery-profile-demo}} with {{fact:recovery-guardians-demo}} required guardians, a {{fact:recovery-delay-ledgers}}-ledger delay and a {{fact:recovery-window-ledgers}}-ledger completion window. Anyone may open an attempt naming replacement credentials for the signers marked replaceable; the controller derives the target document on chain from the current document and that replacement set, so nobody chooses a target hash. Each guardian approves by authorizing a controller call whose one argument is the SHA-256 digest of a {{fact:recovery-statement-bytes}}-byte recovery statement. The statement binds the network, account, controller, configuration, timing and attempt, and the hashes of the source document, the target and the replacement set. The second approval authorizes the attempt at ledger A; completion is then allowed in {{fact:recovery-window}}, from the end of the delay up to, but not including, the end of the window. No recovery can change a replaced credential's kind or verifier, and guardians must check out of band that the replacement keys belong to the person recovering.

Completion is one transaction. The controller's rcv_complete hook confirms the exact approved bytes and the window. Its rcv_sync hook then marks the attempt completed, spends any zero-knowledge nullifier (a one-time tag that stops a proof from being used twice) and advances its epoch, a counter that makes older evidence and attempts stale. The account applies the target document; inserts any new zero-knowledge enrollment; advances the grant generation, refusing on overflow; permanently revokes the administrative credentials the target replaced or removed; lifts any freeze; drops any pending code upgrade; and advances its recovery generation and revision.

The zero-knowledge factor

The zero-knowledge factor proves knowledge of a secret enrolled as a commitment in a depth-{{fact:zk-pool-depth}} membership pool. The proof's public inputs include a Poseidon2 hash (a hash designed for proof circuits) over the account, the enrollment ID and the statement digest the guardians sign, so in combined mode both factors approve one statement. The commitment is public, so the secret must be a uniform field element with at least {{fact:zk-secret-entropy-bits}} bits of entropy.

More on the zero-knowledge factor

The proof is an UltraHonk proof over the BN254 curve; like Ed25519 guardians, the factor is not post-quantum. Built and tested with real proofs, its pool and adapter are in both manifests at {{fact:perch-deployed-source-short}}, and an earlier testnet deployment ({{fact:zk-earlier-deployment}}) exercised every profile and mode in {{fact:zk-earlier-transactions}} transactions; this page exercises guardians alone. The factor gives no anonymity: the commitment is in the public account document, and the pool's insertion event names the account. Its zero-knowledge verifier, Perch's addition to Nethermind's audited non-zero-knowledge UltraHonk verifier, is not audited.
