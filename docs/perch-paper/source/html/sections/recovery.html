A recovery profile, set in the account document, fixes the threat model. Loss assumes the owner's key may be lost but not stolen: ordinary activity, grants included, continues during an attempt, and the owner can cancel any attempt at any time, so a thief holding the owner key can cancel every recovery. Protected assumes the key may be stolen: once the recovery condition authorizes an attempt, the account refuses everything except its completion, and changing the recovery configuration or upgrading the account needs the condition's evidence. The condition is a guardian quorum, a zero-knowledge proof, or both.

§10's experiment uses Loss with two required guardians, a 10-ledger delay and a 100-ledger completion window. Anyone may open an attempt naming replacement credentials for the signers marked replaceable; the controller derives the target document on chain from the current document and that replacement set, so nobody chooses a target hash. Each guardian approves by authorizing a controller call whose only argument is the SHA-256 digest of a 278-byte recovery statement, which binds the network, account, controller, configuration, timing, attempt, and the hashes of the source document, target and replacement set. The second approval authorizes the attempt at ledger A; completion is allowed from A + 10 up to, but not including, A + 110 (Figure 8). No recovery can change a replaced credential's kind or verifier, and guardians must check out of band that the replacement keys belong to the person recovering.

Completion is one transaction. The controller's rcv_complete confirms the exact approved bytes and the window, and its rcv_sync then marks the attempt completed, spends any zero-knowledge nullifier and advances its epoch. The account applies the target document; inserts any new zero-knowledge enrollment; advances the grant generation, refusing on overflow; permanently revokes the administrative credentials the target replaced or removed; lifts any freeze; drops any pending code upgrade; and advances its recovery generation and revision.

Recovery factors

The zero-knowledge factor proves knowledge of a secret enrolled as a Poseidon2 commitment in a depth-32 membership pool; its UltraHonk proof's public inputs include a hash of the statement digest the guardians sign, so in Combined mode both factors approve one statement. Its soundness rests on pairing-based commitments over BN254, so, like Ed25519 guardians, it is not post-quantum. Built and tested with real proofs, its pool and adapter are in both manifests at c135aca, and an earlier testnet deployment (7ae915dc) exercised every profile and mode in 81 transactions; this page exercises guardians only. The factor gives no anonymity (the commitment is in the public account document and the pool's insertion event names the account), and its zero-knowledge verifier, Perch's addition to Nethermind's audited non-zero-knowledge UltraHonk verifier, is not audited.
