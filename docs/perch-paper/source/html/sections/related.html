Grants borrow from capability systems and policy languages; what differs is where a grant lives and what keeps its state.

Capabilities and delegation. An app grant addresses a form of the confused-deputy problem {{cite:confused-deputy}}, in which a program misuses authority it holds on behalf of someone who lacks it. In an account the deputies are the account's callees, which act on the authorization the account gives them, and the owner's execute, which runs with the account as invoker. A grant bounds what an app can have the callees do in the account's name; execute is outside every grant ({{ref:sec:limits}}). SPKI/SDSI certificates {{cite:spki}} narrow authority by intersecting tags, and macaroons {{cite:macaroons}}, Biscuit {{cite:biscuit}} and UCAN {{cite:ucan}} let a holder attenuate a bearer token offline by adding caveats. Perch's grants differ in two ways: they are state the owner installs in the account, not tokens a holder carries, and attenuation is not an on-chain operation at the pinned build. A windowed cap needs a record of past spending, which a bearer token cannot carry without an external store. In Schneider's terms {{cite:schneider-enforceable}}, a Perch cap makes the evaluator an execution monitor with state drawn from the execution's own history; an oz-policy-builder read makes the decision depend on another contract's mutable state, which lies outside that setting.

Account abstraction on EVM chains. ERC-4337 {{cite:erc4337}} accounts validate a user operation in validateUserOp. ERC-7579 {{cite:erc7579}} (draft) standardizes validator, executor and hook modules; its validators and hooks are the EVM counterpart of OpenZeppelin's policies. ERC-7710 {{cite:erc7710}} (draft) specifies an interface for redeeming delegations and leaves their acquisition to other protocols; ERC-7715 {{cite:erc7715}} (draft) specifies how a dApp requests such permissions from a wallet. Zodiac Roles {{cite:zodiac-roles}} lets a Safe give an address a role whose permissions name target contracts and functions and put conditions on call parameters, which is close in shape to a Perch grant. On Soroban the host hands the account every call in the authorized tree, and __check_auth can read the ledger and write state. Perch charges caps in the account's own decision; OpenZeppelin accounts charge them in a policy contract called from __check_auth.

Policy languages. Cedar {{cite:cedar}} combines a policy language, formal models and automated reasoning for authorization; its verification-guided development {{cite:cedar-vgd}} links a Lean model to the Rust implementation by differential testing. Perch's proof object is a functional translation of its restricted Rust evaluator, related to a rule model: a different link to the implementation, which still leaves compiled code and the enclosing system trusted. Cedar, Perch and oz-policy-builder all deny by default. Cedar adds forbid; Perch has no negation; oz-policy-builder has ne and nin, which deny when an operand does not resolve. Cedar keeps no state, while Perch adds explicit rule selection, windowed caps and a generation. In XACML's vocabulary {{cite:xacml3}}, both designs enforce in the account's __check_auth. Perch's decision point is linked into the account. oz-policy-builder's is split between the account's own checks (rule selection, expiry, the signer filter) and its attached policies (the interpreter, spending_limit, any threshold policy), and its read makes other contracts sources of policy information.

Policy synthesis. Synthesising a predicate from a recorded transaction, as oz-policy-builder does, is related to policy mining, which infers access-control policies from logs of permitted actions {{cite:xu-stoller-mining}}. oz-policy-builder itself is compared in {{ref:sec:compare}}.

Soroban contract verification. Certora Sunbeam {{cite:sunbeam}} checks Rust-rule specifications against compiled WebAssembly, and Komet {{cite:komet}} fuzzes and symbolically executes Wasm against K-framework semantics. Perch's repository has Komet properties for its earlier evaluator, perch_program::rpn::eval, not for rule_eval. Both tools complement a proof of a pure rule engine, whose shell and host remain separate targets.
