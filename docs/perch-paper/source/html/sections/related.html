Grants borrow from capability systems, account abstraction and policy languages; what differs is where a grant lives and what keeps its state.

Capabilities. A grant addresses a form of the confused-deputy problem {{cite:confused-deputy}}. SPKI {{cite:spki}}, macaroons {{cite:macaroons}}, Biscuit {{cite:biscuit}} and UCAN {{cite:ucan}} attenuate tokens that a holder carries. A Perch grant is state installed in the account, and a windowed cap needs a record of past spending that a bearer token cannot carry alone. In Schneider's terms {{cite:schneider-enforceable}}, a cap makes the evaluator an execution monitor whose state is the execution's own history.

Account abstraction. On EVM chains, ERC-4337 {{cite:erc4337}} accounts validate each operation, and three drafts, ERC-7579 {{cite:erc7579}} modules, ERC-7710 {{cite:erc7710}} delegations and ERC-7715 {{cite:erc7715}} permission requests, cover similar ground. Zodiac Roles {{cite:zodiac-roles}} is close in shape to a Perch grant.

Policy languages. Cedar {{cite:cedar}} pairs a policy language with formal models; its verification-guided development {{cite:cedar-vgd}} links a Lean model to Rust by differential testing, where Perch proves a translation of the Rust. In XACML's terms {{cite:xacml3}}, Perch's decision point is linked into the account. Synthesising a predicate from a recording, as oz-policy-builder does, is related to policy mining {{cite:xu-stoller-mining}}.

Tools. Certora Sunbeam {{cite:sunbeam}} checks specifications against compiled Wasm, and Komet {{cite:komet}} fuzzes and symbolically executes it; both complement a proof about a pure function. soroban-sim {{cite:soroban-sim}} runs real contract bytes in a portable host, which is what lets this page run both designs.
