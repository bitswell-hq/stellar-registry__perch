ABSTRACT

Perch is a smart-account contract for Stellar's Soroban platform. Its owner installs grants: bounded permissions that let an application act without asking the owner to approve every call. Nido, a passkey wallet, creates Perch accounts. A grant needs a meaning that survives nested contract calls, delegation and account recovery, so Perch makes the authorization decision a pure function linked into the account contract: given the selected rules, their spending records, the calls, the claimed signers, the ledger and a grant generation, it refuses or returns the next spending records. Charon and Aeneas translate the compiler's MIR for that Rust into Lean, where kernel-checked theorems relate the translation to a readable model and establish containment, attenuation and sliding-window cap properties; a separate account model shows that a completed recovery invalidates every earlier grant. The account shell around the function, which installs documents, authenticates signers, stores state and authorizes recovery completion, is tested rather than proven.

We compare this design with our earlier account built on OpenZeppelin's Soroban account library, the Perch build deployed on Stellar testnet and mainnet. The build described here is an open pull-request stack; it runs on a separate testnet deployment behind a Nido preview, and this page runs its contract WebAssembly offline. That a completed recovery ends every earlier grant's authority in the completion transaction is proven in the account model, tested in the shell, executed in this page and recorded on testnet. We propose extending the engine to an agent runtime, where process isolation and complete mediation would remain separate obligations.
