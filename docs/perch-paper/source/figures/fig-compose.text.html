A model of the same five checks in two arrangements. Each request is signed by the app and a co-signer, and the oracle’s price is above the floor, unless it says otherwise. (a) Shaped like a Perch account: one decision point holds the checks and the window record; it has no slot for a live read or a per-call bound: a Perch rule reads nothing outside the account and cannot bound one call’s amount. (b) Shaped like a policy-composed OpenZeppelin account: one policy per check, each with its own state, run in order, starting with the window cap.

  1. Pay 6 to the shop. (a) accepted; written: the window record, now 6 of 10. (b) policies run: window cap, recipient pin, per-call bound, two signers, live read; accepted; written: the window cap’s record, now 6 of 10.
  2. Pay 5 more to the shop. (a) refused at the decision point: window cap (6 + 5 = 11 > 10); nothing written. (b) policies run: window cap; refused by the window cap (6 + 5 = 11 > 10); nothing written.
  3. Pay 1 elsewhere. (a) refused at the decision point: recipient pin; nothing written. (b) policies run: window cap, recipient pin; refused by the recipient pin after the window cap wrote: rolled back with the call.
  4. Detach the window cap in (b); pay 6 to the shop. (a) refused at the decision point: window cap (6 + 6 = 12 > 10); nothing written. (b) policies run: recipient pin, per-call bound, two signers, live read; accepted; no window cap attached, so nothing written; moved in the last 100 ledgers: 12, over 10.

Window at most 10: in (a) while the rule keeps its cap; in (b) while the window-cap policy is attached. Composition buys flexibility; one decision point gives one place to check.
