A grant document becomes stored rules through lowering. Four theorems cover that step: what lowering refuses, what the result allows, that the translation computes it, and that a document's canonical bytes identify it.

The account installs a grant by calling the document compiler, which validates the JSON and returns a model of the document, and then lowering that model with rule-core's lower_scoped under its own address \(A\), its excluded set \(X\) and its current generation \(g\) ({{ref:def:documents-and-lowering}}). In the account, \(X\) holds its adopted recovery controller and the zero-knowledge adapter and pool of its recovery configuration; lowering refuses any rule whose target is \(A\) or in \(X\).

Three hypotheses

  • Doc.Valid: every rule that requires all its signers names distinct signers, fewer than \(2^{32}\) of them, and no rule has a not-after ledger of 0. That Perch's compiler accepts only valid documents is recorded as unproven ({{fact:claim-perch-validation}}; obligation O5).
  • LowerRoom: the document's lists fit the evaluator's bounded vectors, so the translated lower has room to run.
  • ArgsRoom: the weaker condition that each rule's argument conditions fit, enough for the translated lower_scoped, refusals included.
{{statements:T8,T9,T12,T13}}

{{ref:stmt:grant-target-containment}} is the install-time half of containment, and it survives attenuation. {{ref:stmt:the-containment-chain}} composes issue, attenuation and acceptance: what is accepted is allowed by the source document, never calls the account or its excluded set, and is evaluated at the issuing generation. The account builds \(X\) from its storage rather than from the translated excluded, and because a controller can be adopted after a grant is installed, __check_auth rechecks each selected grant rule against the current set; both are tested (obligation O4). {{ref:stmt:lowering-equivalence}} ties the translated lowering to the model, and for a valid document says that the lowered rules allow exactly what the document allows, plus, when recovery is configured, the recovery rule's call, which the account refuses outside a completion (tested; O8).

Canonical bytes

A grant's doc_hash is the SHA-256 of its CANON v1 bytes, Perch's canonical JSON: RFC 8785 {{cite:rfc8785}} restricted to what Perch documents contain. {{ref:stmt:canonical-identity}} is about a Lean emitter in Perch's repository, written to match Perch's Rust canon.rs and linked to it by golden fixtures and fuzzing, not by extraction; it uses Lean {{fact:lean-perch-canon}}, not rule-core's version. No theorem connects this encoding to rule-core's document type, and JSON parsing and compiler correctness need separate assurance. The CANON version is not part of the hashed bytes, so a doc_hash is read under the identity the account reports (capabilities().doc_identity).
