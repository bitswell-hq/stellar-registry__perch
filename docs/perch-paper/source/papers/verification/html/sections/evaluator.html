Five theorems about the decision function: it matches the model on every input, accepts only what the rules allow, accounts for every signer, keeps each capped window within its limit, and keeps delegation-tree budgets.

{{ref:stmt:total-equivalence-of-the-evaluator}} is the bridge for the rest: the translated function succeeds on every input, so it neither panics nor loops, and its result denotes the model's. A property proved for the model therefore holds of the translation's results on denoted inputs. Most theorems below were also proved directly about the translation, and their Lean lines say which. {{ref:stmt:acceptance-implies-permission}} and {{ref:stmt:signer-accounting}} take the signer list as an input; authenticating it is the account's job (obligation O1, {{ref:sec:obligations}}).

Why the second form of Theorem 6 starts at ledger 1

{{ref:stmt:cap-window-safety}} has two forms. The first bounds every window \((\max(0, L - P), L]\) for every ledger \(L\) and needs no assumption about ledgers. The second bounds every \(P\) consecutive ledgers starting at any \(s\), and is proved for the model when every authorization is at ledger 1 or later. At ledger 0 the eviction cutoff \(\max(0, 0 - P)\) is 0, so a record keeps nothing from ledger 0 and each spend there is checked alone. rule-core's Cap.period_ledger_zero records two spends of the whole limit at ledger 0, after which the \(P\) ledgers from 0 hold twice the limit, while every window of the first form still holds at most the limit, since none contains ledger 0. Stellar's ledgers start at 1. The cap-window instance in the card above has a toggle for ledger 0.

The bound on chain

The evaluator keeps at most {{fact:evaluator-history-bound}} entries in a record. On chain the binding limit is the size of one ledger entry: a spending record takes {{fact:spend-record-base-bytes}} bytes plus {{fact:spend-record-per-spend-bytes}} per spend, so the network's {{fact:ledger-entry-limit-bytes}}-byte entry limit holds {{fact:spends-per-window}} spends per window. Further capped transfers through that rule fail until older spends leave the window, so a grantee can exhaust its own rule's window with small transfers. This is a refusal, never an excess: the theorem still holds.

{{ref:stmt:delegation-tree-budgets}} is about rule-core's budget model, not the account: allowances are lifetime totals in a tree whose host mediates every spend. No Perch account runs it; the Perch vision proposes the runtime that would.
