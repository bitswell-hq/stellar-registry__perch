\paragraph{Shared ground, two arrangements.} We cite oz-policy-builder at
\fact{opb-pin-short} from its public documentation and source. Both designs build on OpenZeppelin's
context rules, judge each call alone (\nolinkurl{docs/architecture.md:307-309}) and
deny by default. OpenZeppelin with oz-policy-builder composes a decision from
contracts attached to a rule (interpreter, \code{spending\_limit}, a threshold
policy), each swappable and auditable on its own. Perch puts one document behind one decision
point that holds every check and its state.
Composition buys flexibility, such as a check that reads a live price, which a
Perch rule cannot express; one decision point gives one place to check and to
keep a limit's state.

\paragraph{A limit over time.} The interpreter ``does not see what a transaction
moved, keep rolling totals or count calls'' (\nolinkurl{docs/stellar.md:404-407});
totals come from \code{spending\_limit}, an opt-in policy that accepts
\code{transfer} alone (\nolinkurl{policy-synth/src/install.ts:957-967}). A
Perch window is part of the rule's decision (Theorem~\ref{stmt:cap-window-safety}).

\paragraph{Where each is stronger.} Table~\ref{tab:tab-choose} puts
oz-policy-builder first, and it is ahead in three ways: \emph{maturity} (its
first three rows; Perch's grant system is unmerged, a testnet preview and
unaudited), \emph{authoring} (record or declare, then synthesise and verify a
minimal predicate) and \emph{expressiveness and ecosystem} (reads of other contracts,
quantifiers, clock windows, threshold policies, a deny code per reason). Perch is ahead in \emph{limits over time}, \emph{one decision
point}, which refuses before any verifier runs and installs one grant across
contracts, and a \emph{proved core}; no formal model was found in
oz-policy-builder's tree, which is tested, partly on the shipped Wasm. Perch's
caveats: the proofs cover the translation, and nothing is audited.

\paragraph{Lessons for Perch.} We propose five: recording as authoring, with a
front end that emits a grant document for review; simulating a grant against
the account's own decision function before install, as oz-policy-builder
verifies against \code{check}; minimality checks that flag rules wider than a
recording; a corpus of real transactions run through Perch's lowering and
decision function; and an audit before any claim of maturity. The companion
sets out every dimension with sources.
