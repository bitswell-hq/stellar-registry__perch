\subsection{Further definitions}

\begin{perchdefinition}{D5}{narrowing and derivation}\label{def:d5}
Narrowing $R' \sqsubseteq R$ is as in D2. $\mathit{Derives}(R_0, R)$ holds when
$R$ is reached from $R_0$ by zero or more successful attenuation steps
(Table~\ref{tab:tab-attenuation}). $\mathit{TraceNarrower}(R', R, f)$ holds when
every run that $R'$ accepts from empty records, $R$ accepts from its own empty
records with each choice $j$ renumbered to $f(j)$; a step that drops rule $i$
renumbers every later index down by one.
\leannames{\lean{RuleSet.Derives}, \lean{RuleSet.TraceNarrower}}
\end{perchdefinition}

\begin{perchdefinition}{D6}{documents and lowering}\label{def:d6}
For a document $d$ on account $A$, $\mathit{Doc.allows}(d, A, c, S)$ holds when
some rule of $d$ covers $c$'s contract and function, its argument constraints
hold and its principals are signed by $S$; no rule allows a creation.
$\mathit{Doc.Valid}(d)$ holds when every rule that requires all its signers names
distinct signers, fewer than $2^{32}$ of them, and no rule has a not-after ledger
of~0. $\mathit{lower}(d, A)$ is the rules of an account document, followed by the
recovery rule when $d$ configures recovery. With
$X = \mathit{excluded}(d_{\mathrm{admin}}, A)$, which is $A$, the adopted
controller and, if configured, the zero-knowledge adapter and pool,
$\mathit{lowerScoped}(d, A, X, g)$ refuses a document that configures recovery
or has a rule whose target is $A$, a member of $X$ or the account's
self-administration, and otherwise lowers $d$'s rules stamped with generation
$g$. rule-core's conditions, \fact{rule-core-conditions}
(Table~\ref{tab:tab-attenuation}), are an equality, a set membership, a string
prefix and a signed upper bound. \code{is-self} and \code{address-eq} lower to an
address equality, \code{u32-eq} to an equality on the argument's encoding,
\code{string-in} to a membership and \code{string-prefix} to a prefix; a cap adds
the upper bound of the largest \code{i128} on argument~2, which is a type
guard.
\leannames{\lean{Doc.allows}, \lean{Doc.Valid}, \lean{Doc.lower}, \lean{Doc.lowerScoped}}
\end{perchdefinition}

\begin{perchdefinition}{D7}{account events}\label{def:d7}
An account state is $s = (A, d_{\mathrm{admin}}, g)$. The event
$\mathsf{applyDoc}(d)$ sets $d_{\mathrm{admin}} := d$; the event
$\mathsf{completeRecovery}(t)$ sets $d_{\mathrm{admin}} := t$ and $g := g + 1$,
and is refused when $g$ is the largest \code{u64}. A run applies events in
order, a refused one leaving $s$ unchanged. A grant document $d$ is
\emph{issued} in $s$ as $\mathit{lowerScoped}(d, A,
\mathit{excluded}(d_{\mathrm{admin}}, A), g)$.
\leannames{\lean{Account.step}, \lean{Account.run}, \lean{Account.issue}}
\end{perchdefinition}

\begin{perchdefinition}{D8}{host run}\label{def:d8}
A model of soroban-env-host running a transaction of contract calls under
enforcing authorization. Every call is a frame that a failure rolls back.
\code{require\_auth} of an address holds when the frame's direct caller is that
address (invoker authority), or when an authorization entry of the address
matches; on its first match the address's \code{\_\_check\_auth} runs on the
entry's contexts in preorder, then the expiration is checked and the nonce
consumed. Stellar-account signers, \code{require\_auth} inside
\code{\_\_check\_auth}, \code{require\_auth\_for\_args},
\code{authorize\_as\_current\_contract}, contract creation as a call and
source-account credentials are outside the model.
\leannames{\lean{Soroban.Tx.run}}
\end{perchdefinition}

\begin{perchdefinition}{D9}{denotation}\label{def:d9}
$\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket$ maps the translation's values (byte vectors,
\code{u32} and \code{u64} scalars, rules, caps, records, contexts, signers and
outcomes) to the model's. \ref{stmt:total-equivalence-of-the-evaluator} and
\ref{stmt:lowering-equivalence} are stated through it: for every input $x$ there
is a result $y$ with $\mathit{authorize}_{\mathrm{T}}(x) = \mathsf{ok}(y)$ and
$\llbracket y\rrbracket = \mathit{authorize}(\llbracket x\rrbracket)$.
\leannames{\lean{Equiv.Denote}}
\end{perchdefinition}

\paragraph{Ending authority.} A grant is \emph{invalidated} when the account's
generation passes the generation it was stamped with; it stays stored and refuses
every nonempty batch. \emph{Removal} (\code{remove\_grant}, by the owner) deletes
a grant's rules, tags and spending records. \emph{Collection}
(\code{gc\_grant}, by anyone) deletes the storage of an invalidated grant and
keeps the credential index. \emph{Archival} is a lapse of an entry's time to
live: the entry is unavailable until restored, not absent.

\paragraph{The period window at ledger 0.} The second form of
\ref{stmt:cap-window-safety} assumes every authorization is at ledger~1 or later.
At ledger~0 the eviction cutoff $0 \mathbin{\dot-} P$ saturates to~0 and evicts
entries at ledger~0, so each spend at ledger~0 is checked alone: rule-core's
\lean{Cap.period\_ledger\_zero} records two spends of the whole limit at ledger~0,
and the $P$ ledgers from~0 then hold twice the limit. Stellar's ledgers start
at~1.

\subsection{Canonical identity and signature binding}

\paragraph{CANON v1.} \code{doc\_hash} is the SHA-256 of a document's CANON~v1
bytes. The CANON version is not part of the hashed bytes, so a \code{doc\_hash}
is read under the identity the account reports
(\code{capabilities().doc\_identity}, which is \code{canon\_v1}).
\ref{stmt:canonical-identity} proves the encoding injective on the modeled
document type; \lean{configPreimage\_ne\_emitDoc} is the one proven domain
separation, between the recovery-configuration preimage and an emitted document.

\paragraph{Binding.} Let $P$, $B$ and $D$ be as in \S\ref{sec:authorization}.
The following is a paper proof, not mechanized.

\begin{lemma}[binding]\label{lem:binding}
Assume the external signer's signature scheme is existentially unforgeable under
chosen-message attack and $H$ is collision resistant. If a verifier accepts a
signature on $D$, then, except with negligible probability, the signer signed
$D$ for exactly one tuple $(A, \mathit{nid}, \mathit{nonce}, \mathit{exp},
\mathit{inv}, \rho)$, and the signature authorizes no other account, network,
nonce, expiration ledger, invocation tree or list of rule IDs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The map from the tuple to $D$ composes injective encodings with $H$. The XDR
preimage of $P$ is self-delimiting; $\tau$ is a constant, and $\mathit{id}_A$ and
$P$ have fixed lengths, so $(A, P) \mapsto \tau \| \mathit{id}_A \| P$ is
injective; $B$ has a fixed length and the XDR of $\rho$ is self-delimiting, so
$(B, \rho) \mapsto B \| \mathrm{XDR}(\rho)$ is injective. Two distinct tuples
with one $D$ therefore give a collision of $H$ at one of its three applications.
Otherwise an accepted signature on $D$ for a tuple the signer did not sign is a
forgery on a message the signer never signed. \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[non-binding]\label{prop:non-binding}
$D$ does not depend on the contents of the rules $\rho$ names, on the account's
revision, on the other entries of the transaction or on its fee payer. For a
delegated signer, the payload the host has it approve depends on the account but
not on $\rho$.
\end{proposition}

\noindent This follows from the preimages: none of these values enters $P$, $B$
or $D$, and the delegated payload is the host's address-bound preimage.

\paragraph{Addresses.} A Soroban contract ID commits to its deployer and salt,
not to its code. The claim that an account calls fixed infrastructure code
therefore assumes that the registry deploys each pinned Wasm hash at the salt
derived from it and that the deployed infrastructure contracts cannot replace
their code.

\subsection{Cryptographic assumptions and domain tags}

\paragraph{Schemes.} Ed25519~\cite{rfc8032} signatures are checked with strict
verification. P-256 signatures are ECDSA with low $S$ enforced; the WebAuthn
verifier~\cite{webauthn2} checks neither the relying-party ID nor the origin.
\fact{mldsa-verifier} is pure FIPS~204~\cite{fips204} with a fixed context
string, the key being the hash of the public key. The zero-knowledge factor
uses Poseidon2 (width~4) and an UltraHonk proof with KZG commitments over BN254
against a fixed setup point, and Fiat--Shamir with Keccak; \code{docs/zk/} does
not name the setup ceremony. BN254 and Ed25519 are not post-quantum, so an
\fact{mldsa-verifier} signer protects only what its rule alone can authorize.

\paragraph{Domain tags.} Perch separates its hash domains with the tags
\code{\fact{auth-domain-tag}} (the account-bound payload $B$, scheme
\fact{auth-scheme-name}), \fact{domain-tag-rule} (one rule's bytes),
\fact{domain-tags-recovery} (recovery statements, configurations, credentials
and replacement sets), and, as Poseidon2 constants, \fact{domain-tags-zk}. CANON
document hashes carry no tag. For comparison, oz-policy-builder uses
\fact{opb-domain-tags}.

\paragraph{Recovery statement.} The \fact{recovery-statement-bytes}-byte
statement a guardian approves consists of \fact{recovery-statement-layout}. Its
digest is the SHA-256 of these bytes; the action byte precedes the subject and
selects its layout, so the encoding is injective without length prefixes.
