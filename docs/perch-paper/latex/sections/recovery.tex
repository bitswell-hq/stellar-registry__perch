The account document's recovery profile fixes what recovery defends against.
\emph{Loss} assumes the owner's key may be lost, not stolen: activity continues
during an attempt, and the owner, or a thief holding the key, can cancel it.
\emph{Protected} assumes theft: once the recovery condition authorizes an
attempt, the account refuses everything but its completion, and changing
recovery or upgrading needs the condition's evidence. The condition is
a guardian quorum, a zero-knowledge proof, or both (modes \fact{recovery-modes}).

The reference run uses \fact{recovery-profile-demo} with
\fact{recovery-guardians-demo} guardians, a \fact{recovery-delay-ledgers}-ledger
delay and a \fact{recovery-window-ledgers}-ledger window. Anyone may open an
attempt naming replacement credentials; the controller derives the target
document on chain, so nobody chooses a target hash. Each guardian approves the
SHA-256 digest of a \fact{recovery-statement-bytes}-byte statement binding the
network, account, controller, configuration, timing, attempt and the hashes of
source, target and replacement set. The second approval authorizes the attempt
at ledger $A$; completion is allowed in \fact{recovery-window}. Completion is
one transaction: the controller's \code{rcv\_complete} confirms the approved bytes
and the window, and \code{rcv\_sync} marks the attempt completed, spends any
nullifier and advances the controller's epoch. The account applies the target,
advances the grant generation (refusing on overflow), permanently revokes the
administrative credentials the target replaced or removed, lifts any freeze,
drops any pending upgrade, and advances its recovery generation and revision
(Fig.~\ref{fig:fig-recovery}).

\paragraph{Ending authority.} Each earlier grant keeps its earlier generation, so
it refuses every nonempty authorization while its rules remain stored. A
credential index refuses (\code{WipedCredential}) any later document naming a
credential an earlier grant named, except the recovery target, which may keep it
until rotated; replaced administrative credentials go into a separate, permanent
revoked set. Table~\ref{tab:tab-ending} separates invalidation, a policy event,
from collection, a storage operation, and archival, which is neither;
Table~\ref{tab:tab-counters} (Appendix~\ref{sec:formal-appendix}) lists the
account's four counters.



\paragraph{Zero-knowledge factor.} It proves knowledge of a secret enrolled as a
Poseidon2 commitment in a depth-\fact{zk-pool-depth} pool; its UltraHonk proof's
public inputs include Poseidon2 over the account, the enrollment ID and the
statement digest, so in combined mode both factors approve one statement. The
commitment is public, so the secret must be a uniform field element with at
least \fact{zk-secret-entropy-bits} bits of entropy. The factor works over BN254,
so, like Ed25519 guardians, it is not post-quantum, and its zero-knowledge
verifier has not been audited.
